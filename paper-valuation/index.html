Is the forecast holding?

ReadingAbove all three cases, too early to count Trend since the forecastBetter ThesisHolding no test has resolved yet, no kill line crossed Next check20 Oct: first reading of the payout-bar test · 28 Oct: Update 2

Three days after the forecast was frozen, Papertrade is making more money, not less: $6.3M a day on 14–16 Oct against the $4.45M a day the forecast started from, with 91% of it paid to stakers against the 85% assumed. The market's bet moved up too: Polymarket's median one-day-after-launch value is now near $200M, up from about $100M, on fourteen times the volume. Two things went against us: PAPER supply is growing faster than the model assumed (8.45B on the live contract, not the 7.2B modelled for today), and DefiLlama revised down the three days the reference was built on. None of it changes the published call. The first real test starts when the pool's payout bar doubles to $10M, after 18:45 UTC on 17 Oct.

How to read this: the forecast of 14 Oct is frozen. Every block below marks it against what actually happened, in the same order and the same words every time. The full update with every source, as Markdown: update 1. Raw data: the Tracking section below.

3. What we got wrong, and what changed under us

  1. The reference was built on figures the feed later revised. DefiLlama now shows 12 Oct at $3.93M (was $4.65M) and 13 Oct at $4.52M (was $6.08M): the same three days give $3.69M a day, 17% below the frozen $4.45M. Same method on the revised figures: $0.41 / $0.069 / $0.0091. The call stands as made; the miss is recorded.
  2. The supply tuning was too low. The model set the curve's high-water mark at 3.0 × revenue; the live contract shows 4.0, and 8.45B PAPER against 7.16B modelled for today. Frozen inputs with 4.0: $0.48 / $0.078 / $0.0096 (−2% to −8%). Best reading of why: the curve counts gross flow into the pool, not net losses. An inference from the contract's tailProgress; the docs still cannot be read as text.
  3. The 17 Oct test was timed from each case's run-rate, not the actual one. At today's pace the pool refills in a day, so its first reading will not separate the cases. The reading that counts is how long surplus payouts stay paused once the rush fades, from mid-November on.
  4. Two doubts in the report are resolved; neither was a model input. The "$910B daily volume" on 13 Oct was cumulative (the day itself was $73.7B). The 30.8 PAPER per $1 that did not fit the published curve fits it once the curve runs on gross flow.
  5. "Market bet $0.016" divided $100M by 6.1B modelled supply. On live supply the same strike is $0.012, and the new median near $200M is $0.024.

Details · farming

When does getting PAPER stop being worth it?

PAPER is only created when someone loses a trade. Each $1 lost earns fewer new PAPER as more exist. Once 1 PAPER costs more to get than it's worth, chasing it stops paying.

      How the math works
      • Daily revenue today × share a similar token kept = future daily revenue.
      • × 365 × share paid to stakers = yearly cash to stakers.
      • × the multiple buyers paid for that similar token = value of all PAPER.
      • ÷ PAPER that will exist by then = value per PAPER.
      • Supply rule: 100 new PAPER per $1 lost until the pool reaches $2M, then fewer as more is created, heading toward about 12B.
      • Farming stop line = cost to get $1 of loss ÷ value per PAPER. Below that many PAPER per $1, it costs more than it's worth.
