3. What we got wrong in Version 3

  1. The reference slid again. Version 3 asked whether revenue would "hold near $1.7M a day". It did not: 24–26 Oct averaged $0.68M, 60% lower, so the forecast fell again: base −54% on the old curve, −57% with the new mint rule. The three days include 25 Oct, the quietest day since launch ($72.8K). A seven-day reference (20–26 Oct, $0.77M) would give a base of $0.016, close to the $0.014 the method gives: the question of the window (still with Insaaf) matters less now than it did a week ago, when the gap was $0.074 against $0.034.
  2. "Tracking closest to: bull" is built to say bull. The bars each case needs are cut from the same reference that just fell, so recent revenue almost always clears them. Against Version 1's fixed bars, the last seven days ($0.77M a day) sit nearest the base case ($0.44M; the bull needed $1.51M). The page keeps its rule; the fixed-frame reading is stated here and lined up for Insaaf with the reference question.
  3. The throttle tell broke within a day of being made. We set the tell at 15× when the daily reads had run 7.7× to 12.3×. The lean moves a lot within a day: the job replaced the 20 Oct morning read (10.2×) with an afternoon read (17.9×), and 21 Oct read 18.4×. The call is missed and stays missed; the lean has since fallen to 4.95× because the bets shrank, not because the house grew.
  4. We missed the upgrade for six days. The daily checks of 22–27 Oct said "no sign of the upgrade" because they watched the old curve's fields: the cap, the old formula's mint rate and its high-water mark. The mark froze at $263.3M on 21 Oct precisely because the curve it drives was retired; we read the freeze as "no change". The checks now read the new constant and the timelock's on-chain events (24 hours' notice of any owner change, including the payout bar).
  5. Version 3's supply curve had a ceiling the contract no longer has. On the old curve the rule sat 7% under live supply (8.44B against 9.03B). The new rule has no ceiling and mints a share of whatever exists, so the bull case, whose revenue mints the most, ends Oct 2027 at 12.2B PAPER instead of 10.2B. Re-run on the old curve, today's inputs give $0.095 / $0.015 / $0.0016: the method change moves the cases 17%, 7% and 6% down.
  6. The mint-rate call hit on a curve that was retired hours later. It marked itself hit on the 21 Oct read (9.93 per $1). Under the new rule the rate rises with supply: 9.94 today, back above 10 once supply passes 9.08B. The hit stands; its premise, a rate that keeps falling, does not. Every case's farming stop line (bull 12.7 per $1) is now above the rate, so losing on purpose no longer pays even in the bull case; Version 3 had the bull paying until about 28 Dec.
  7. The market's bet is an upper bound now. The rule takes the strike nearest 50%. Every strike now sits under 50% ($25M and $50M both 45.5%), so the median the ladder implies is below $25M; $25M is kept by the same rule and should be read as "at most".

What we got wrong in Version 2 (written with Version 3)
  1. The reference caught the peak. Version 2's three-day reference ($6.29M, 14–16 Oct) was the three best days after launch day; three days later the same method reads $1.72M. Because the method re-bases on the newest three days, the forecast swung +40% and then −70%. A seven-day reference (13–19 Oct, $4.08M) would give a base of $0.074: a shadow run, not the forecast. Whether the reference should widen is lined up for Insaaf; the method stands until he decides.
  2. The 17 Oct tell was a dip between sweeps, not a pause. We wrote that stakers "have had only the 1% win fee since about midnight". The pool reads under the bar whenever traders are ahead on open bets; it crossed back over the bar in three hours that day (sweeps of $22K, $324K and $251K) and stakers received $783K, 87% of the day's revenue.
  3. Two hits rest on a rule that measures the wrong thing. The restart calls marked themselves hit on 18 Oct from holders ÷ revenue (90%). That ratio is the split of distributed revenue between stakers and the treasury; it read 85–95% on every day since launch, and the bar never doubled, so there was nothing to restart. The hits stand as the rule scored them (nothing is hand-edited); the replacement rule (the pool reading at the bar on the daily contract read) is lined up for Insaaf.
  4. The test was written as if the doubling were certain. The tracking card said "from 17 Oct the pool refills to $10M first" and the key dates carried refill dates from 17 Oct. It did not happen. Both now follow the contract read, and the refill dates apply from the day the bar moves.
  5. Two small errors in the record. The call sheet's static text said "+36%" where the computed figure was +40% (the page corrected itself on load). A forwarded note put 16 Oct revenue at $6.37M against the API's $5.45M. Neither reached the model.
  6. A doubt resolved. The docs can be read now: the curve's high-water mark is cumulative pool gain past $2M (tailProgress), which confirms Version 2's inference that the curve runs on gross flow. The supply tuning read 4.2 on 20 Oct.

5. The throttle: the house against the bets

  • 1Fees are relabelled, not gone. A trade costs nothing to open, but the house keeps a cut of every winner's profit: a haircut of 10–50%, largest on small moves, plus a 2% win fee split between stakers and the team. The 19 Oct note adds a fee band on trades closed within 6–10 basis points of entry, aimed at bots. Since 21 Oct it is live, with builder codes taking 0.5% of profit; volume fell from $19B a day to $5.5–7.8B, which fits bots being priced out, though farmers leaving pushes the same way.
  • 2The bar is the dial. The house is the pool plus its reserve: $8.0M on 27 Oct (pool $4.91M, reserve $3.13M), all a trader can win, against $164M of open bets. Everything the pool holds above the $5M bar is swept to stakers, so every raise of the bar is "house first", bought with stakers' income. The raise to $10M announced for 17 Oct has still not happened, ten days on, and the team has not said why.
  • 3House first, or rewards first. Insaaf, 17 Oct 2026: traders must be able to get in and out at size; fees are a negligible part of a trader's decision; a platform should put trading and the house before LP rewards, and "if the house and trading experience are in order, rewards will follow". House first is bearish for PAPER for a few months and bullish for next year. The base case is the house-first world. No new thoughts from him reached this version.
  • 4The desk's tempering. Seamless entry at size against a zero-impact house is the known exploit path (the $1.28M ETH incident of 11 Oct; GMX on Avalanche in 2022), which is why the team refused transferability on 19 Oct: a PAPER short would let an attacker bet against the bankroll. Today's traders are mostly loss-miners, so execution decides the organic cohort that must replace them, and that cohort is the retention input every case rests on. The tell we check daily: how far the bets lean against the house money.
  • 5Where the data agrees and disagrees, a week on. Agrees: the house held. The queue stayed at $0 and every winner was paid, even on 21 Oct, when the bets leaned 18.4× the house money and the house gave back about $2.7M; the reserve took the hit. Disagrees, so far: the dial still reads rewards first. The pool kept sweeping to stakers above $5M while the reserve stayed about $2M below its 20 Oct level ($3.13M against $5.00M), and the bar did not move. Nor has trading grown into the space: open bets fell from $315M to $164M, open positions from 3,003 to 1,549, money on platform from $63.8M to $31.5M. The lean eased to 4.95× because the bets shrank, not because the house grew. Bearish now, as the thesis says; "rewards will follow" and "bullish next year" are untested, because house first has not been tried.

Details · farming

When does getting PAPER stop being worth it?

PAPER is only created when someone loses a trade. Each $1 lost earns fewer new PAPER as more exist. Once 1 PAPER costs more to get than it's worth, chasing it stops paying.

      How the math works
      • Daily revenue today × share a similar token kept = future daily revenue.
      • × 365 × share paid to stakers = yearly cash to stakers.
      • × the multiple buyers paid for that similar token = value of all PAPER.
      • ÷ PAPER that will exist by then = value per PAPER.
      • Supply rule, from Version 4: since the contract upgrade of 21 Oct 2026, each $1 lost mints a fixed share of the PAPER that exists (about 0.110% per $1M), with no ceiling. Versions 1–3 used the first rule: 100 new PAPER per $1 lost until the pool reached $2M, then fewer as more was created, heading toward about 12B.
      • Farming stop line = cost to get $1 of loss ÷ value per PAPER. Below that many PAPER per $1, it costs more than it's worth.
