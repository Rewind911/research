## 3. What we got wrong

1. **One day moved the forecast 64%.** Version 5 published base $0.0094 on 10 Nov. A day later the same method gives $0.015, because 10 Nov ($999,535) came into the three-day window and Saturday 7 Nov ($60,180) left it: reference +72%, bull +49%, base +64%, bear +70%. Over the last 13 closing days the three-day reference has ranged from $0.038M (31 Oct–2 Nov) to $1.03M, 27×. Version 5 named this fragility its sharpest lesson and still published the three-day number as the forecast; that is the method until Insaaf decides (BOARD 18), and this version does the same. Shadow run, not the forecast: a seven-day reference (4–10 Nov, $0.5926M) gives bull $0.070, base $0.012, bear $0.0013.
2. **Its next question was answered the other way within a day.** Version 5 asked whether revenue would "hold near $0.45M a day" by 17 Nov; the next closed day was $1.00M and the seven-day average is $0.59M. The weekly question was framed on the same three-day number.
3. **It quoted readings the data file no longer holds.** Version 5's throttle text and "What moved" table gave the 10 Nov 06:47 UTC read (house $8.10M, open bets $50.8M long and $29.8M short, lean 2.59×). GitHub's late run replaced that row at 14:49 UTC (house $8.52M, $37.0M long and $23.7M short, lean 1.56×), so by evening the chart under the text showed different numbers (BOARD 23). This version quotes the job's rows and names the read time when it uses the desk's own.
4. **The two data fixes are still not done.** The job's `mint_rate` still prints the retired curve's 9.80 (10.24 by the live rule; the page computes the right figure), and there is still no daily read of the timelock. Promised with Version 5, carried to 17 Nov (BOARD 25, 26).
5. **Small things.** Version 5's supply-tuning note said the days since Version 4 alone give 3.9; with one more day they give 3.66, so that check moves as much as the input it checks (3.7 → 3.5). The bear call was "on track" partly because Version 5's base ($0.0094) sat just under $0.011; today's base ($0.015) sits above it, so that evidence weakened; the call stays on track on the nearest-value reading.

Every input was re-read against its source today; no revision was found to any day Version 5 read (revenue or holders, 10 Oct–9 Nov).

## The throttle, three and a half weeks on

Insaaf's thesis (17 Oct 2026): traders must be able to get in and out at size; fees are a negligible part of a trader's decision; a platform should put trading and the house before LP rewards; "if the house and trading experience are in order, rewards will follow". House first is bearish for PAPER for a few months and bullish for next year; the base case is the house-first world. No new thoughts from him reached this version.

The desk's tempering: the house held and is growing back. The queue read $0 on every day, including 5 Nov, when house money fell to $5.91M; on 11 Nov it read $8.96M, the most since 30 Oct, with the reserve at $3.97M (from $2.72M on 5 Nov). The lean has stayed under 15× on every read since 22 Oct (1.82× today). But the dial still reads rewards first: the bar is still $5M, 25 days after the raise to $10M was due, and the pool keeps sweeping above it. Trading has not grown into the space: open bets $145M (27 Oct row) → $68.2M (11 Nov row), open positions 1,516 → 993, money on platform $31.5M → $24.0M. The lean is low because the bets shrank. Bearish now, as the thesis says; "rewards will follow" is still untested, because house first has not been tried.
