## 3. What we got wrong

1. **The reference slid again.** Version 3 asked whether revenue would "hold near $1.7M a day". It did not: 24–26 Oct averaged $0.68M, 60% lower. On the old curve that alone takes the base from $0.034 to $0.015 (−54%); with the new mint rule it is $0.014 (−57%). The three days include 25 Oct, the quietest closed day since launch ($72.8K). Version 3's reference days were not revised by the feed ($898,397, $2,338,673, $1,914,597 as read on 20 Oct and today). The window question is still with Insaaf (BOARD 18), but it matters less now: a seven-day reference (20–26 Oct, $0.77M) gives base $0.016 against the method's $0.014, where a week ago the gap was $0.074 against $0.034.
2. **The desk missed the upgrade for six days.** The daily checks of 22–27 Oct said "no sign of the upgrade" because they watched the old curve's fields: the cap, the old formula's mint rate (computed by our job from the high-water mark) and the mark itself. The mark froze at $263.3M on 21 Oct precisely because the curve it drives was retired; we read the freeze as "no change". The team never posted that the upgrade was queued, though it said it would; the chain shows it (scheduled 20 Oct 21:35 UTC, executed 21 Oct 22:00 UTC). Consequence: the version that should follow the upgrade within a week is this one, on day six. Fixed: the daily-check brief now reads `rebateC36` and the timelock's events, which give 24 hours' notice of any owner change, the payout bar included. The job's `mint_rate` field still computes the retired curve (BOARD 25).
3. **Version 3's supply curve had a ceiling the contract no longer has.** On the old curve the rule already sat 7% under live supply (8.44B against 9.03B). The new rule mints a share of whatever exists, with no ceiling, so the bull case, whose revenue mints the most, ends Oct 2027 at 12.2B PAPER instead of 10.2B. Consequence in numbers: today's inputs on the old curve give $0.095 / $0.015 / $0.0016; on the new rule $0.079 / $0.014 / $0.0015 (−17%, −7%, −6%).
4. **The mint-rate hit rests on a curve retired hours later.** `mint-10` marked itself hit on the 21 Oct 11:44 UTC read (9.93 per $1); at 22:00 UTC the curve was replaced. Under the new rule the rate rises with supply: 9.80 at the switch, 9.94 today, back above 10 once supply passes 9.08B. The hit stands as scored (nothing is hand-edited); its premise, a rate that keeps falling as farming fades, no longer holds.
5. **The bull case's farming date was two months out.** Version 3 put the bull stop line at 4.9 PAPER per $1, around 28 Dec 2026. Now every case's stop line is above the 9.94 minted per $1 (bull 12.7): losing on purpose no longer pays even in the bull case.
6. **"Tracking closest to: bull" is built to say bull.** The bar each case needs after the rush is cut from the same three-day reference that just fell, so the last seven days almost always clear even the bull bar ($0.77M against $0.23M today). Against Version 1's fixed bars (bull $1.51M, base $0.44M, bear $0.09M a day), the last seven days sit nearest the **base** case. The page keeps its rule (changing the tracking line is a question of design for Insaaf, added to BOARD 18); this note and the Scorecard state the fixed-frame reading next to it.
7. **The throttle tell broke within a day of being made.** We set it at 15× when the reads had run 7.7× to 12.3×. The lean moves a lot within a day: the job's afternoon run replaced the 20 Oct morning read (10.2×) with 17.9×, and 21 Oct read 18.4×. Consequence: a missed call, kept. The lean has since fallen to 4.95× because the bets shrank ($315M → $164M), not because the house grew ($9.8M → $8.0M).
8. **The market's bet is an upper bound now.** The rule takes the strike nearest 50%. Every strike now sits under 50% ($25M and $50M both 45.5%), so the median the ladder implies is below $25M; $25M is kept by the same rule as "at most". Conditional on a token existing, the sweep's arithmetic puts the median between ~$54M and ~$240M depending on an untraded launch quote: too wide to use.
9. **Doubts resolved.** The timelock is 24 hours (docs; on-chain schedule-to-ready gap exactly 24:00:00), not IOSG's 7 days. Version 3's day-by-day table used the 20 Oct 06:47 contract read (10.2×); the record now holds the 11:31 read for that day (17.9×). Both are shown here; nothing published was edited.
